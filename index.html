X-Ray

What is an X-ray?

An X-ray uses a small, controlled dose of radiation to take a still picture of the inside of the body. It is quick, painless and widely used.

Common uses: fractures and joint problems, chest and lung conditions (pneumonia, TB), dental and sinus views, abdomen and spine.

Preparation: remove metal items (jewellery, belts). Tell the staff if you may be pregnant.

Video X-Ray

What is a video X-ray?

Video X-ray (fluoroscopy) shows live, moving images of organs and joints, so the doctor can watch function in real time.

Common uses: swallowing studies, barium swallow/meal, joint movement, guiding injections and catheters.

Preparation: some studies need fasting or a contrast drink. Staff will give instructions before the test.

Safety

Doses are kept as low as reasonably achievable. Pregnant women should always inform the radiographer before any imaging.
